\begin{abstract}
Reading interfaces could adapt to whether users read for comprehension or seek
specific information.  Recent work combines fixation-level gaze with
contextual text, requiring fixation-to-word alignment and a language encoder
at inference.  We instead use a word-aligned teacher only during training to
supervise a GRU that receives the chronological fixation sequence.  On
OneStop, with readers and articles both held out, distillation raises balanced
accuracy by 3.39
percentage points over the same GRU trained with hard labels.  The improvement
remains positive under reader-clustered and crossed reader/article bootstrap
analyses.  Shuffling teacher targets within each class reduces the gain by
1.90 points but does not eliminate it.  Adding contextual text to the
word-level teacher yields no detectable student benefit.  On one laptop CPU
thread, median and 95th-percentile forward-pass latencies are 5.08 and
16.57~ms.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
eye tracking, scanpath analysis, reading behavior, human--computer interaction,
sequence classification
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

A reader may approach the same passage to understand it broadly or to locate
specific information.  These goals are associated with differences in
fixation durations, regressions, and scanpath structure
~\cite{rayner1998,shubi2023information}.  An opt-in classifier could support
navigation or content assistance after each paragraph~\cite{shubi2025decoding}.
For this use, a model should generalize to readers and articles absent from
training.

Shubi \emph{et al.} combined fixation-level gaze with contextual text to
classify reading goals for unseen readers and articles
~\cite{shubi2025decoding}.  At inference, this model requires text,
fixation-to-word alignment, and a language encoder.  We ask whether
word-aligned supervision can instead be used only during training while
prediction uses gaze alone.

We study teachers that aggregate gaze by word.  One teacher also receives
contextual word representations.  Their soft predictions supervise a
student that observes the chronological fixation sequence.  The primary
comparison isolates the training signal by holding the student architecture
and input fixed.  At inference, the student uses no teacher, text encoder,
tokenization, or fixation-to-word alignment.  Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}
summarizes this separation between training and inference.

Each OneStop reader completes only one goal condition, while articles recur
across readers.  We therefore hold out readers and articles simultaneously and
estimate uncertainty by resampling readers, with crossed reader/article
resampling as a sensitivity analysis.

This study makes three contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item With student inputs and architecture fixed, teacher-derived soft
  targets improve the gaze-only GRU when both readers and articles are unseen.
  \item Matched ablations assess the roles of teacher modality and the pairing
  between each training trial and its teacher prediction.
  \item We characterize student size and single-thread CPU latency across the
  observed sequence-length distribution.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Eye Movements and Reading Goals}

OneStop includes eye movements from ordinary reading for comprehension,
information seeking, and repeated reading~\cite{berzak2025onestop}.  It has
also supported gaze-only and text--gaze models for predicting reading
comprehension under new-reader and new-item conditions
~\cite{shubi2024comprehension}.

Shubi \emph{et al.} introduced this reading-goal decoding task under new-reader
and new-item conditions and evaluated gaze-only and text--gaze models at
fixation and word levels~\cite{shubi2025decoding}.  We use a word-level teacher
to place contextual text and aggregated gaze in a common representation.  This
supports a controlled removal of text while the student's chronological
fixation input remains fixed.

\subsection{Distillation with Privileged Information}

Knowledge distillation (KD) trains a student with class labels and a teacher's
temperature-softened predictive distribution~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
Generalized distillation allows the teacher to use privileged information that
is unavailable to the student at inference~\cite{lopezpaz2016unifying}.  In our
setting, the teacher uses fixation-to-word alignment and may also receive
contextual text, whereas the student receives only the chronological fixation
sequence.  We test whether the benefit depends on contextual text or on the
pairing between each training trial and its teacher prediction.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Task and Data}

The unit of analysis is a completed paragraph-reading trial represented by its
eye movements.  The binary target is the experimentally assigned reading-goal
condition: ordinary reading for comprehension ($y=0$) or information seeking
($y=1$).  In the information-seeking condition, readers preview the question
before the paragraph.  In ordinary reading, readers see the question only
after reading the paragraph.  Readers
were randomly assigned to one condition~\cite{berzak2025onestop}.  We use the
anonymized public release, collected with consent under an institutional ethics
protocol, and retain first readings from these two conditions.  We exclude
repeated readings, question-screen events, answers, correctness, and all
post-reading information.

The resulting data contain 19,438 trials from 360 readers and 30 articles, with
nearly balanced goal labels.  Each reader completed one goal condition, and the
median trial contains 96 valid fixations.  Reader identity, article identity,
question content, session position, and condition metadata are not model
inputs.

Each fixation is encoded by ten features: $x$ and $y$ coordinates normalized to
the paragraph bounds, log fixation duration, horizontal and vertical
displacement from the preceding fixation, and log saccade amplitude.  The
remaining features are the sine and cosine of the saccade angle, a
line-transition indicator, and position within the observed sequence.  Retained
fixations have positive finite durations and finite coordinates.  The first six
features are standardized using statistics from the current training set only.
The angular encodings, line-transition indicator, and sequence position retain
their native scales.

\subsection{Models}

Table~\ref{tab:models} summarizes the five neural model variants.  Models
trained without distillation minimize binary cross-entropy (BCE).  \modelStats{}
maps 22 sequence summaries to a two-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) with
widths 128 and 64, ReLU activations, and dropout 0.2.  The summaries cover
duration, displacement, saccade amplitude, line transitions, spatial
dispersion, and path length.

\modelGru{} applies layer normalization to each fixation and processes the
sequence with a two-layer unidirectional GRU~\cite{cho2014gru} with hidden size
64.  A 64-unit ReLU layer with dropout 0.2 maps the final GRU state to the
output logit.  This model is the matched undistilled baseline for
\modelDistill{}.  Sequences are
zero-padded within each minibatch, and the classifier uses the GRU output at
the trial's final fixation before padding.

A frozen RoBERTa-base encoder produces contextual subword
representations~\cite{liu2019roberta}.  For each paragraph word,
\modelTeacher{} averages its subword representations.  Eight word-level gaze
features encode fixation count, total and mean dwell time, first-fixation and
first-pass dwell time, regression-in and revisit counts, and skipping.  Text
and gaze features are projected to 128 and 64 dimensions, concatenated with a
32-dimensional learned word-position embedding, and mapped to width 192.  A
learned classification token is prepended before a three-layer, six-head
Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017attention} with feed-forward width 384, GELU, and
dropout 0.1.  A linear head maps the classification-token representation to the
output logit.  A padding mask excludes padded word positions.
\modelText{} retains the text branch and fusion architecture but sets all gaze
features to zero.  It tests whether paragraph text alone predicts the reading
goal.

\modelDistill{} has exactly the same architecture and inputs as \modelGru{}.
Let $z_S$ and $z_T$ be the student and teacher logits, and let $\tau=2$.  The
student minimizes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_D={}&(1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{BCE}(y,\sigma(z_S))\\
&+\alpha\tau^2\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left[
q_T^{(\tau)}\,\middle\|\,q_S^{(\tau)}\right],
\quad q_j^{(\tau)}=\mathrm{Bern}\!\left(\sigma\!\left(\frac{z_j}{\tau}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kd}
\end{equation}
In the primary analysis, \modelDistill{} uses targets from \modelTeacher{}.
The word-aligned gaze teacher is evaluated as a post hoc control in
Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}.
Each teacher produces in-sample logits for the outer-training trials on which
it was fit.  These logits are used only to train the student.  No held-out test
trial is used to fit either model or construct a distillation target.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

OneStop assigns each reader to one of three article batches.  Readers are
divided into five folds stratified by goal and batch, and articles are divided
independently into five folds.  Every reader fold contains both goals from all
three batches.  The Cartesian product produces disjoint test partitions that
each contain both goals and together cover all retained trials once.  For each
reader--article fold pair, training excludes every trial from either held-out
fold, and the test set is their intersection.  All test readers and articles
are therefore absent from training.

Within each outer training set, model selection uses three paired inner
validation splits.  Each validation split likewise holds out both its readers
and articles from the corresponding inner training set.  Learning rates are
selected from
$\{10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-4}\}$ for the MLP and GRU models and
$\{3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-4}\}$ for the teacher models.  We also select
$\alpha\in\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}$ for the objective in~\eqref{eq:kd}.  Balanced
accuracy is the selection metric, with negative log-likelihood (NLL) as the
first tie-breaker.
Each final model is refitted for the median number of epochs selected across
the three inner splits.  The decision threshold is fixed at 0.5 before test
evaluation.  Test labels are not used for early stopping, calibration, or
threshold selection.

Models are optimized with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, and gradient clipping at 1.0.  Inner training uses at most 30 epochs
with a patience of five epochs.  Batch sizes are 512 for \modelStats{}, 256 for
the GRU models, and 128 for the teacher models.  Final refits use the selected
learning rate and fixed epoch count described above, without test-dependent
stopping.

\subsection{Baseline and Ablation Controls}
\label{sec:controls}

We used the same outer splits and training setup for the following post hoc
controls.  Paired conditions inherit the corresponding model's selected
learning rate and epoch count and keep the remaining student, teacher, or KD
settings fixed.

The first control replaces KD with binary label smoothing
($\epsilon=0.10$)~\cite{szegedy2016label}.  The second creates a word-aligned
gaze teacher by zeroing every RoBERTa representation while retaining the
architecture and word-level gaze summaries.  Across three random seeds, we
refit the BCE and label-smoothed GRUs, both teachers, and both KD students.
Each student uses logits from the teacher trained with the same seed.

The third control cyclically reassigns teacher logits within each ground-truth
class.  This preserves each class-conditional distribution while removing the
pairing between a training trial and its teacher prediction.  It uses the same
seeds and student settings.

We also fit logistic regression (LR) controls using either log dwell time per
word~\cite{shubi2025decoding} or the 22 gaze summaries.  Their regularization
is selected within the same nested reader/article splits.

\subsection{Metrics and Uncertainty}

Balanced accuracy (BA) is the primary metric because it averages the two
goal-specific recalls~\cite{brodersen2010balanced}.  Secondary metrics are
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), macro F1,
negative log-likelihood (NLL), Brier score, and 15-bin expected calibration
error (ECE)~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

For the primary contrast between \modelDistill{} and \modelGru{}, a paired
5,000-draw percentile bootstrap resamples whole readers within each goal.  We
report the central 95\% interval.  A 2,000-draw sensitivity analysis resamples
readers within each goal and independently resamples articles, weighting each
trial by the product of their multiplicities.
Bootstrap samples are paired between models.  For each ablation contrast,
resampled units are paired across conditions, and the seed-specific BA
differences are averaged within each draw.

\subsection{Model-Only Latency}

We benchmark one distilled checkpoint on every retained fixation sequence.
After 100 warm-up iterations, we time one full-sequence forward pass per trial
in randomized order.  Measurements use an Intel Core Ultra 7 155H and one
PyTorch thread, excluding input construction, parsing, and disk I/O.

\subsection{Model-Only Latency}

The distilled and undistilled GRUs have identical parameter counts and
inference operations.  The serialized student state occupies 176~KiB.  Across
all retained sequences, model-only latency has a 5.08-ms median and a 16.57-ms
95th percentile.

\section{Discussion}

Teacher-derived soft targets improve the matched gaze-only GRU relative to
hard-label BCE.  The gaze-summary logistic baseline remains competitive, and
the distilled model's advantage over it is uncertain under crossed
reader/article resampling.

Trial-level target pairing explains part of the gain, but not all of it.
Shuffling within class reduces performance, while shuffled targets remain above
BCE.  The residual improvement may reflect class-dependent targets or an
optimization effect.  The tested label-smoothing setting does not yield a
similar improvement, and adding contextual text produces no detectable student
benefit.  Both teachers also share word-level gaze aggregation, greater
capacity than the student, and in-sample target generation.  These properties
remain possible sources of the gain.

Bootstrap intervals characterize sampling uncertainty for fixed out-of-fold
predictions, while the seed analysis covers three initializations.  Because
each reader was assigned one goal, the study does not test within-reader goal
changes.  Generalization beyond complete scanpaths from English news read with
a laboratory eye tracker remains open.

The gaze-only student still requires calibrated coordinates and paragraph
bounds, and the CPU benchmark measures model-only forward passes.  Any
adaptive use requires evaluation with users.

\section{Conclusion}

Training-time distillation improves a gaze-only GRU for unseen readers and
articles while inference uses only fixation sequences.  Within-class target
shuffling reduces the gain, whereas adding contextual text to the tested
word-level teacher yields no detectable benefit.  Together, these results
support word-aligned teacher supervision as a training-time method for
improving a fixation-sequence GRU without changing its inference inputs or
operations.  This separation is relevant to opt-in reading interfaces because
prediction requires neither contextual text nor fixation-to-word alignment at
runtime.
